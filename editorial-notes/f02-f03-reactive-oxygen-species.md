# Reactive oxygen, two questions

Both F02 and F03 ask whether an applied physical stimulus is associated with a redox-related signal: Sherrard and colleagues report intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) in cultured cells exposed to low-intensity electromagnetic fields, while Arany and colleagues connect low-power laser treatment with ROS, latent transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), and dental repair. That shared phrase is a useful starting point—not yet evidence that the studies saw the same chemical or mechanism. ([Sherrard et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.2006229); [Arany et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3008234))

The consequential difference is the experimental question. F02 asks about a cryptochrome-associated cellular response to a magnetic-field exposure; its authors propose spin-dependent radical-pair chemistry, but the archived account does not show that the proposed spin process was directly observed. F03 asks whether light can activate an endogenous latent growth-factor signal in a dental-regeneration context. The archived claim associates ROS with TGF-β1 activation and reports preclinical dental outcomes, but its source record is only abstract-checked. In the material currently available, neither record identifies an assay that would let us name a particular ROS, establish its subcellular location, or align sampling times. “ROS” may therefore be a broad assay signal or mechanistic inference, not a shared molecular actor.

This comparison remains provisional: the original methods, figures, and supplements were not accessible through the tools in this session, so assay specificity, cell and animal species, exposure schedules, controls, and timescales still need paper-level checking. The two systems also differ—cell culture under pulsed fields versus light-triggered signaling in preclinical dental models—and neither, on this record, establishes a human treatment. **Editor’s interpretation:** the juxtaposition is valuable as a lesson in how one umbrella term can conceal unlike observables, rather than as evidence for one quantum-biological pathway. What species-selective measurement and causal perturbation would show that either study’s ROS signal is necessary—and, more ambitiously, that the two signals are chemically and mechanistically comparable?
